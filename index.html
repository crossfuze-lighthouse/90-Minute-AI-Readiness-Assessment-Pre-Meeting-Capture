How to read these scores

This benchmark places your organisation on a five-stage curve from your own answers. It is a fast, structured read — a strong starting point for the conversation, not the final word.

Your stage is the honest one. Each stage rests on the one below it, so you only reach a level once the foundations under it are genuinely in place. You cannot score "Run" on an unmanaged platform — which is exactly how maturity works in practice.
Read the pillar scores as bands, not decimals. The four pillars sit on different numbers of questions, so treat them as "strong / mixed / exposed" rather than precise percentages. The shape across the four matters more than any single figure.
It reflects what you told us. The scores are only as accurate as the answers behind them. Where you were unsure, the live session is where it gets confirmed.
The gaps are the value. The point isn't the number — it's the specific statements holding your next stage back. Those become the agenda for a 90-minute readiness session.

Briefing notes for presales. This is a hypothesis to verify in the live session, not a finding to present as settled.

Gating model. A unit's level is the highest stage clearing the 70% readiness gate, walked bottom-up — defensible on sequence. Use it as the spine of the conversation.
Pillar base imbalance. Pillars pool answers across all units into a percentage, but rest on uneven bases (Data ~5 statements, Governance ~13). One Data answer swings that ring far more than one Governance answer — read Data as a band, don't quote it to the decimal.
"Partly" is weighted at half. A partial scores 1 of 2. Confirm this matches how the client means it — "bought but not usable" versus "working but not optimised" changes where the value line falls. Watch for capabilities that are partly in place everywhere: usually licensed and half-deployed, which is the CoreEssentials conversation.
Self-assessed, especially cross-function. A CIO answering for Legal or Finance is often estimating. The survey gives you the lay of the land; the session verifies it and goes deeper. Treat surprising scores as questions, not facts.

How this benchmark works

A directional reference, not a validated ranking. It shows roughly where a readiness score like yours would sit relative to the market — useful for orientation, confirmed in the live session.

What your score measures. It is the average of the statements you answered, scored out of the maximum, across the four pillars and five stages. Yes counts full, Partly counts half, Not yet counts zero.
It reflects only what you assessed. The score covers the functions you benchmarked in this run, not every part of the enterprise. Functions you did not score are not in the number.
The scales are aligned, not identical. Your Crossfuze readiness score and ServiceNow's published AI Maturity Index are two different instruments. We place one against the other for reference, so treat the position as indicative — a band, not a precise percentile.
Why it is still useful. Even as a directional read, it shows whether you are broadly ahead of, level with, or behind the market — and where the value line sits. The precise placement is confirmed in the 90-minute readiness session.
